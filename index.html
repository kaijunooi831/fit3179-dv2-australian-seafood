Australia’s
seafood shift

Farming reshaped Australia’s seafood industry. See how farming and wild catch changed, which parts of Australia produce the most value, and where exports go, including Malaysia.

New to this topic? Aquaculture means farming fish and other aquatic products; wild catch comes from natural waters. A state or territory is a part of Australia, such as Tasmania or Western Australia. Production value is what output is worth when it leaves a farm or fishing boat, not its shop price or profit.

Reading the numbers. A$ means Australian dollars; A$m means millions of Australian dollars, and “bn” means billion. A tonne is 1,000 kilograms. A 2024–25 financial year runs from July 2024 to June 2025.

Not every state or territory moved toward farming.

For each place, the percentage is farmed value divided by farmed value plus wild catch managed there. Tasmania rose from 33.5% to 91.4%; Western Australia fell from 31.7% to 18.9%.

Aquaculture’s share, then and now

Tasmania made the clearest shift toward farming. Its share rose from 33.5% to 91.4%, while Western Australia moved in the opposite direction. A rising percentage does not necessarily mean more total production.

A few groups carry
much of the value.

Salmonids (salmon and trout) accounted for 34.0% of national industry value in 2024–25. Their importance is even greater within aquaculture, where they contributed 60.3%.

Exports are concentrated.
The connections reach Malaysia.

Australian fisheries exports were worth A$1.48 billion as they left the country in 2024–25. Rock lobsters, salmonids and tunas made up 75.4% of that value.

Australia’s seafood industry
is more than a catch total.

Recorded farmed production value now exceeds wild catch, with much of farmed value in Tasmania and salmon and trout. A few product groups dominate export value; the selected destination data show strong Asian links, including Malaysia. These dollar patterns do not tell us whether seafood is affordable, profitable or environmentally sustainable.

Where the numbers come from

Two data sources

ABARES Australian fisheries and aquaculture statistics, September 2026 tables, supplies production and export values through preliminary 2024–25.

ABS Consumer Price Index, July 2026, Table 17, supplies the inflation figures used to compare past production values in 2024–25 dollars.

What the totals include

ABARES calls production value gross value of production (GVP). It includes non-food products such as pearls, but excludes hatcheries. The A$3.72 billion Australia-wide total removes tuna counted in both wild catch and farming; it is not the sum of the two sector lines.

Missing or confidential figures are never treated as zero. A published zero may mean an amount too small to appear at the source’s precision.

Geography, trade definitions and licences

State and territory outlines come from Australian Bureau of Statistics (ASGS 2021); the country map comes from Natural Earth. Australian maps use an equal-area projection; the world map uses Equal Earth. Map points show whole places, not exact farms, ports or catch sites.

Exports are valued at the border; production is valued at the farm or first landing. Their dollar amounts cannot be added to estimate domestic seafood use.
