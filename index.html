Australia’s seafood shift

Farming reshaped Australia’s seafood industry. See how farming and wild catch changed, which parts of Australia produce the most value, and where exports go, including Malaysia.

New to this topic? Aquaculture means farming fish and other aquatic products; wild catch comes from natural waters. A state or territory is a part of Australia, such as Tasmania or Western Australia. Production value is what output is worth when it leaves a farm or fishing boat, not its shop price or profit.

Reading the numbers. A$ means Australian dollars; A$m means millions of Australian dollars, and “bn” means billion. A tonne is 1,000 kilograms. A 2024–25 financial year runs from July 2024 to June 2025.

Farmed value grew.
Wild-catch value shrank.

In 2024–25 dollars, aquaculture value grew 67.6% between 1998–99 and 2024–25. Wild-catch value fell 48.7%.

Farmed and wild production took different paths

Farmed value first overtook wild catch in 2019–20 within this series. The Australia-wide total, adjusted so tuna is not counted twice, was 14.7% lower than in 1998–99 after inflation. Over the latest decade, however, it rose 2.5%.

Values are adjusted for inflation to 2024–25 dollars. This accounts for general price rises, making years easier to compare. The national total removes tuna counted in both sectors, so it is not their sum.

Not every state or territory moved toward farming.

Farming share is farmed value as a percentage of farmed plus locally managed wild-catch value.

Choose a place to highlight it; lines show endpoints only. Seven places have reported values; the Australian Capital Territory has none.

Tasmania made the clearest shift toward farming. Its share rose from 33.5% to 91.4%, while Western Australia fell from 31.7% to 18.9%. A rising share does not necessarily mean more total production.

Aquaculture’s share, then and now

How did the typical farming share change?

Farming became more common, but regional differences remained. Across seven states and territories, the median farming share rose in each of the four snapshots, from 32.4% in 1998–99 to 52.6% in 2024–25. The latest shares range from 18.9% to 91.4%.

Salmonids (salmon and trout) accounted for 34.0% of national industry value in 2024–25. Their importance is even greater within aquaculture, where they contributed 60.3%.

Salmonids dominate farmed value across the decade

Salmon and trout stand apart throughout the decade. In 2024–25 they supplied 60.3% of farmed value, up from 55.1% in 2015–16; oysters supplied 8.3% and prawns 8.1%. The source also includes pearls and grouped products.

Exports are concentrated.
The connections reach Malaysia.

Australian fisheries exports were worth A$1.48 billion as they left the country in 2024–25. Rock lobsters, salmonids and tunas made up 75.4% of that value.

Where the numbers come from

Two data sources

ABARES Australian fisheries and aquaculture statistics, September 2026 tables, supplies production and export values through 2024–25.

ABS Consumer Price Index, July 2026, Table 17, supplies the inflation figures used to compare past production values in 2024–25 dollars.

What the totals include

ABARES calls production value gross value of production (GVP). It includes non-food products such as pearls, but excludes hatcheries. The A$3.72 billion Australia-wide total removes tuna counted in both wild catch and farming; it is not the sum of the two sector lines.
